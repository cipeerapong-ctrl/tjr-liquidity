## P4.1 What prop firms are and why he recommends them
- P4.1 Opening claim: funded accounts are a big tool for low-capital traders to scale capital fast, by paying a small fee to trade a much larger account through a challenge. He says most beginners start with little money, and there is no shame in that. (00:00–00:43)
- P4.2 Definition he gives: a prop firm is a company you pay a small fee to; it gives you a trading challenge in which you must hit a profit target without losing more than a set amount (the drawdown). (00:43–01:04)
- P4.3 Each firm has its own rules and several account types. Some firms have no challenge at all: you are funded straight away, but the payout rules are stricter. (01:04–01:26)
- P4.4 Typical account sizes he lists: $25,000, $50,000, $100,000, $150,000. His point: most beginners cannot deposit that much into a normal brokerage account, so the firm gives access to that size of capital, but you must first show you can trade it by passing the challenge. (01:26–02:20)
- P4.5 Price tiers: costlier accounts tend to be easier to pass and to have a higher payout cap; cheaper accounts are harder to pass and have a tighter payout cap. His example: a $50,000 account for about $79 (firm not named) is a harder route and a smaller payout. (02:31–03:34)
- P4.6 He frames it as paying money to prove you are profitable. (03:34–03:44)

## P4.2 His warnings (skill first)
- P4.7 Before buying accounts "to make $2,000 or $5,000", understand that not many people pass the challenges, and for a good reason: you genuinely have to be a good trader. (03:44–04:18)
- P4.8 He says a brand-new viewer following the series (he refers to the previous 12 days) is not ready yet: strategy, risk management, entries and exits have not been covered. (04:18–04:39)
- P4.9 The prerequisite skill: predicting with high probability where price will go on a daily basis (he links this to the "what is trading" episode). Without it you cannot pass a challenge, and you cannot make money on a live, demo or prop account either. He stresses being a good trader as the one requirement. (04:50–05:34)
- P4.10 The challenge fee is real money: a failed account is lost money, and people only realise this after they blow it (his example amount: $279). The risk-management episode will cover how to manage risk when paying for these accounts. (08:54–09:26)

## P4.3 Firm 1 — Alpha Futures (as shown on its website)
- P4.11 Why he likes it: the firm advertises the largest payout cap. He says the industry norm is about $1,200–$5,000 maximum withdrawal and Alpha Futures offers $15,000 per standard or advanced account (his reading of the firm's page; not verified). It is one of his top recommendations. (05:44–06:26)
- P4.12 Plans: Zero (no activation fee; $50,000 and $100,000 sizes), Standard and Advanced. Each has different rules for passing and for payouts. He says none is simply better; it depends on your trading style and goal. (06:26–07:08)
- P4.13 Most people trade the Advanced plan; he says people across the industry call these accounts some of the best because of the large payout. (07:08–07:39)
- P4.14 Advanced $100,000 evaluation as he reads it from the page: minimum 2 trading days; profit target 8% ($8,000); maximum 10 contracts per trade (he says that is plenty unless trading seconds charts); maximum drawdown $3,500, meaning equity may not fall to $96,500 or the challenge is failed. (07:39–08:54)
- P4.15 No profit split during the challenge; a split only applies on the later qualified account. (09:26–09:46)
- P4.16 Reset after failing costs the same as the original fee ($279 in his example; the page shows $279 as the Advanced 100K price and as its reset fee); no daily loss limit. (09:46–10:07)
- P4.17 He calls Alpha one of the more expensive firms, justified by the largest payout cap and a big consistency rule. He says most firms use 30–40% and Alpha uses 50%; in his reading this means the winning profit has to be spread over at least three trades to pass, so no single trade may exceed 50% of the profit. (10:07–10:49)
- P4.18 Trading through news is allowed. (10:49–10:59)
- P4.19 Promo: code TJR gives about 10–20% off Alpha Futures depending on the account (he says he forgot to mention this earlier). (14:24–14:45, 18:38–18:49)

## P4.4 Firm 2 — Tradeify (as shown on its website)
- P4.20 Promo (said before the firm's accounts are described): he says Tradeify made a one-time code TJR40 for this video, 40% off every account type (examples: Growth 50K, Select 150K, Lightning 50K), one use per person (not per account), probably valid only until the end of that day, "the biggest sale they have done". After that, code TJR gives 30% off. (11:33–12:39, 18:49–19:24)
- P4.21 He says Tradeify's best accounts are the instant-funded ones; many people choose between Select and Lightning. (12:39–13:01)
- P4.22 Select accounts allow daily payouts. His claim, unverified: on a $150,000 Select account you could pass the evaluation in 3 days and be payout-eligible in 4, so starting a challenge on Monday and being paid by Friday, which he says almost no other firm allows. (13:01–13:32)
- P4.23 Lightning accounts have no evaluation at all: you pay a one-time fee and are funded immediately. He explains the usual industry flow (pay, take challenge, pass, move to a live account, request payout) and contrasts it with this instant model. (13:32–14:24, 14:45–14:55)
- P4.24 He warns that "no evaluation" does not mean you can be a bad trader: payout rules still require skill. Lightning rules he names: payout every 5 days, maximum five accounts, a 20% consistency rule (roughly five or six trades needed to make up the amount you request). (14:55–15:47)
- P4.25 His comparison (his own estimate): on Alpha Advanced, passing needs at least 3 trades (50% rule) and the qualified account has no consistency rule but needs 5 trading days; so, doing the bare minimum, about 4 trades to a payout. On Tradeify Lightning about 6 winning trades are needed. His conclusion: the amount of trading and risk to reach a first payout ends up roughly the same either way. (15:47–17:11)
- P4.26 His preference: Select (daily payouts) over Lightning. He says many people like Lightning anyway, particularly consistent traders: it has a decent daily loss limit, drawdown and contract count, up to five accounts can be stacked, and you skip worrying about the evaluation. He calls himself unbiased, while also promoting the codes above. (17:11–17:51)
