## P12.1 Frame of the episode
- P12.1 Follow-up to the previous episode (liquidity sits above highs and below lows). This one is about "advanced draws on liquidity": stronger, higher-probability highs and lows than an ordinary random high or low. (01:04–02:08)
- P12.2 He says there are seven forms of advanced draws, but the episode actually walks through five groups: session highs/lows, previous-day highs/lows, low-resistance (stacked) highs/lows, relative equal highs/lows, and data (news) highs/lows. (01:14, 02:08, 25:06, 34:03, 44:17, 51:01)

## P12.2 Session highs and lows
- P12.3 Three sessions in the 24-hour day: Asia, London, New York (New York is further split into AM and PM, which he does not use here). (02:29–02:39)
- P12.4 Why sessions matter: each session brings new money from a different region into the market, and he treats each as its own set of market makers who move price their way. (04:15–04:47)
- P12.5 Mechanism, London vs Asia: to move the market, London money has to stop out the Asia traders. If London wants price higher, it pushes under the Asia low (Asia buyers are stopped, new sellers enter), fills its own buy orders there, then pushes up through the Asia high and sells into the buy orders resting above it. He repeats that above a high sit two sets of buy orders. (05:40–07:47)
- P12.6 Mechanism, New York vs London: New York pushes above the London high, fills sell orders while London traders are stopped out and new buyers enter, then pushes down to the London low where it buys back its shorts cheaper. (07:58–08:53)
- P12.7 Conclusion: Asia high/low, London high/low and New York high/low are all significant draws, because new money arriving at a session start or end targets them for the resting orders above and below. (09:15–09:58)
- P12.8 Times, TradingView on Eastern (New York) time: Asia starts 18:00; London starts 03:00 and runs to 08:30; New York pre-market starts 08:30 and the New York open is 09:30. In his closing summary: Asia 18:00–03:00, London 03:00–08:30, New York 08:30 back to 18:00; together this is a full trading day. (11:19–11:54, 20:01–20:33)
- P12.9 He uses a session indicator that draws each session's high and low plus session-start lines (link in the video description); on the chart he removes the high/low lines and keeps only the session starts so the example can be shown live. (10:30–11:19)
- P12.10 Example 1 (NQ1! 30m, a New Year's Eve day he says he picked at random): London highest/lowest points are measured from 03:00 to 08:30, Asia from 18:00 to 03:00. At the London open price pushes under the Asia low, then runs up to take the Asia high; then New York takes the Asia and London highs and targets the London lows. (12:04–14:34). Frames: NQ1! 30m, session-start lines for 31 Dec 2025; blue circle around the lows under the Asia low, red circle around the high above.
- P12.11 He says this does not happen every day. (14:24–14:55)
- P12.12 Example 2: same sequence: London pushes under the Asia low, fills buy orders, runs to the Asia high; at New York pre-market price goes above those highs, and the New York side then looks to target the lows. (15:49–18:21). Frames: NQ1! 30m, the session of Fri 26 Dec 2025 (Asia from Thu 25 Dec 18:00, London 03:00, NY 09:30); circles on the low under the Asia low and on the high above.
- P12.13 Recap: Asia highs/lows matter because London money can push beyond them and reverse; London highs/lows matter for the same reason to New York money, in either direction (sweep the lows and target the highs, or sweep the highs and target the lows). (18:54–19:51)
- P12.14 Caveat: a push above or below a session level only gives the market the OPPORTUNITY to reverse; it does not have to. Confirmation confluences are not covered yet and come later. (14:45–15:39)

## P12.3 Previous-day high and low
- P12.15 Previous-day high and low are the same idea with all sessions merged: the highest and lowest point of Asia + London + New York combined is the daily high and low. These are also important draws. (20:44–21:18)
- P12.16 Example (the most recent day, daily levels shown): in London price goes above the previous-day high with little motion; at New York pre-market price pushes above the London/Asia highs and back above the previous-day high, then targets the London lows, the Asia lows and the previous-day low. (21:40–23:41). Frames: he first opens ES1!, then NQ1!; he shows the daily chart for previous-day levels, then NQ1! 30m around the NY open of Fri 2 Jan 2026.
- P12.17 Not foolproof: he does NOT say sell every break of a previous-day high or buy every break of a previous-day low; it only gives higher-probability levels that price tends to target. (23:41–24:02)
- P12.18 General logic: for the market to go lower it first has to take a high; the orders it filled there are then offloaded under lows (London low, Asia low, previous-day low). How to pick take-profit levels is deferred to a later episode. (24:12–24:54)

## P12.4 Low-resistance liquidity (stacked highs/lows, trend liquidity)
- P12.19 Low-resistance liquidity = stacked-up highs or lows; he also calls it trendline liquidity or trend liquidity. (25:06–25:59). Frames 25:50–27:50: a hand-drawn staircase of higher lows on a zoomed-out ES1! chart with the top circled; on-screen title text lists "session highs and lows" and "previous day high and low" (25:50).
- P12.20 In an uptrend there is a staircase of higher lows. After price takes a high and sell orders are filled, those stacked lows are the natural targets, because below each low sit people willing to sell, which lets the sellers buy back lower for profit. They are lows not yet manipulated. (25:59–27:16)
- P12.21 Cookie analogy: price will not stop at the first low when another one lies beyond it; market makers want as much as possible, so they keep taking each stacked low. (27:16–28:40)
- P12.22 Mirror in a downtrend: after the reversal begins, price takes the lows and then the whole row of stacked highs above, each with resting orders to run through. (28:40–29:35)
- P12.23 Use it both ways: (a) if you have an entry confluence after lows are swept, use the stacked highs above as exits / take-profit points; (b) if price has just taken out a whole row of stacked lows, the sell orders there are used up, which gives the opportunity to fill large buy orders and push the other way. (29:56–31:21, 31:33–32:07)
- P12.24 Session highs/lows, previous-day highs/lows and low-resistance levels can each serve as entry points and as exit points; e.g. after New York takes the London highs, the session lows are a good exit. (32:34–34:03)
- P12.25 Example A (stacked lows, shown on chart): at the New York open price takes the previous-session (London) highs and the Asia high, then dumps through five stacked lows in a row. Session high = entry point; the stacked lows = exits, one after the other. (34:36–36:15). Frames: ES1! 1h, the New York open of Fri 10 Oct 2025; a red hand-drawn line runs along the stacked lows under the run-up, with a red circle at the high, and horizontal lines from each low.
- P12.26 Example B (opposite direction): a downtrend of lower highs; price reverses upward and takes out the stacked highs, then goes down to take the row of low-resistance lows. Here the stacked highs are the entry level and the stacked lows are the targets. (36:33–37:50). Frames: ES1! 4h, late 2025 (exact date not read), lower highs with horizontal lines drawn from each high.

## P12.5 Relative equal highs and lows
- P12.27 Same idea as low-resistance liquidity, but two highs (or two lows) that are exactly equal in price or just under/over each other. (38:00–38:21, 34:03–34:14)
- P12.28 Why it is stronger: for exactly equal highs, he counts four times the buy orders: above each high there are two sets (traders stopped out of shorts, plus traders buying the break); two highs means two sets stacked on two sets. Same for lows. (38:21–39:14)
- P12.29 Example (15-minute chart): two lows about 50 cents apart, which he accepts as "close enough"; price takes them and he calls it four times the buy orders below, then price pushes back above the highs. (40:03–41:31). Frames: NQ1! 15m, around mid-December 2025 (exact date not read); two lows with a horizontal line drawn from them.
- P12.30 Dead-equal highs are hard to find; he says equal levels appear much more on low time frames. He then switches to the 1-minute chart: two lows whose wicks are at the same price, combined with a third low, which also counts as low-resistance. Price then reverses and takes all those lows. (42:10–43:54). Frames: NQ1! 1m on Fri 2 Jan 2026 (about 10:30–10:50 ET), red circle around the two equal lows.
- P12.31 He says the timeframe does not matter for the concept; it appears on every time frame. (34:36, 43:32–43:54)

## P12.6 Data (news) highs and lows
- P12.32 Data highs/lows = the high and low of the candle(s) that print on a high-impact news release. (44:17–44:27, 45:57–46:07)
- P12.33 Find events on Forex Factory, red-folder items; risk management for news comes later in the series. Example: CPI comes out at 08:30 ET, one hour before the open. (44:27–45:11). Frames: Forex Factory calendar open in the browser; CPI is listed as a red-folder event.
- P12.34 Why: news candles are drastic on low time frames because the data moves the Nasdaq and S&P 500; the high and low of the news candle are treated like other high-probability draws. (45:33–46:07)
- P12.35 Example 1 (CPI, 18 December): works out poorly; he says that is not a reason to give up since levels only give the opportunity. (46:20–47:15). Frames: NQ1! 30m then 5m on Thu 18 Dec 2025; price moves sharply up after the 08:30 bar.
- P12.36 Warning: do not sell just because price pushes above a news-data high or London high, and do not buy just because price pushes under a news-data low or London low; you need other confluences to confirm direction. (47:15–47:36)
- P12.37 He marks the high and low of the news candle on the chart as a draw on liquidity. (47:36–48:11)
- P12.38 Examples 2–3 (PPI 25 Nov: not much movement; unemployment claims 20 Nov): there the news-candle high was not used as the reversal point; a higher-time-frame high was taken, price reversed, and the news-data low was then used as a take-profit. He admits it is not a great example and asks viewers to take his word. (48:11–50:16). Frames: Forex Factory for 25 Nov and 20 Nov 2025; NQ1! 5m on Thu 20 Nov 2025 around the 08:30 release.
