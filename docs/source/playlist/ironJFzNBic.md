## P3.1 Why bias comes first
- P3.1 Daily bias = knowing where the market is likely to go that day. He covers it before risk management and entries, because without it there is no reason to enter. (00:00–00:33)
- P3.2 The episode combines what earlier episodes taught (trend, liquidity, fair value gaps, equilibrium) and walks through five real days. It is explicitly NOT about entries; he only hints at entries on the 5-minute chart. (00:46–00:57, 07:10–07:52, 24:27–24:38)

## P3.2 The bias method — his stated order (spoken, not written on screen)
- P3.3 Step 1, higher time frame trend: is the higher time frame making higher highs / higher lows (uptrend) or lower highs / lower lows (downtrend)? On screen the higher time frame is the 4h (and the 1h for a clearer view). (00:57–01:18, 02:56–03:07, 09:33–09:54, 18:35)
- P3.4 Step 2, draws on liquidity: are there good draws on liquidity (a stack of lows below, or highs above) in the direction of that trend for price to run to? (01:18–01:51, 03:07–03:17)
- P3.5 Step 3, current price action: look at the latest swing and ask what price must do next. After a high and a lower low, price must retrace. The two retrace ("continuation") confluences are a fair value gap and equilibrium; he says either or both can be used. (03:17–03:50)
- P3.6 Step 4, respect or disrespect: if the trend is real, price respects the gap / equilibrium and then runs to the draws. If price closes through the gap instead, that trend is not holding and the bias changes. (02:35–02:56, 03:40–04:01, 08:27–08:58, 29:24–29:47)
- P3.7 His one-line summary of the method: find the most recent liquidity sweep, or whether liquidity is being swept right now; then look at the higher time frame imbalances and equilibrium levels inside the current trend. (26:23–26:44)
- P3.8 Bias then names the target (the draw), not the entry. Entries are left to a later episode. (00:11–00:33, 24:27–24:38)
- P3.9 Order of looking: higher time frame trend and draws first; then the retrace area (gap / equilibrium); then low-time-frame confirmation (5m break of structure after a sweep) only as a preview of entries. (02:56–03:50, 13:41–14:14, 28:41–29:24)

## P3.3 How liquidity connects to the bias
- P3.10 "Draws on liquidity" = low-resistance stacked lows or highs; they serve as targets and can also serve as entry areas inside a trend. (01:18–01:51)
- P3.11 A sweep of liquidity means orders were filled there. Price then needs to go to the opposite side where resting orders sit so that position can be closed; this is why a swept low points to the highs above (his "reverse psychology" framing). (05:25–06:28, 12:29–13:20)
- P3.12 To reach a high-side draw, price first needs a sweep of lows to fill orders (and the reverse for a low-side draw). He says traders are not smart enough to buy right under the low, but can catch the move afterwards. (12:29–13:41, 16:26–17:19)
- P3.13 After a bias is set, he immediately lists the draws on the other side: in example E, the Asia session high and the previous day high are the two targets. This is the only place sessions appear as levels. (29:57–30:19)
- P3.14 Sessions as manipulation windows: at the open he looks for some form of manipulation, coming from the London session, the Asia session or the New York open; hourly or 5-minute manipulation of nearby lows is what he waits for. No session times are given. (10:38–10:58, 11:54–12:05, 20:21–20:31)

## P3.8 How a trend moves: external to internal
- P3.36 Uptrend: from a high down into a fair value gap / equilibrium, then back to a high, and so on; he calls this external to internal to external. Downtrend is the mirror: internal to external, with bearish confirmation sending price down to the lows. (24:48–25:51)
- P3.37 After price pushes under a low it is likely to retrace up into a gap or equilibrium and then come back down to take that low; he calls this the ebb and flow of the market. (25:51–26:23)
