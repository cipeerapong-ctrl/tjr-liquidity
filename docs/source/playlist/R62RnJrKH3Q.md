## P15.2 Why every concept must come with a reason
- P15.6 For every concept he teaches he needs the reason: why use it, what it means, why execute an order at a given place; the reason is that orders are not being filled, or that a trend is continuing. (00:55–01:26)
- P15.7 He criticises support-and-resistance style strategies (a ceiling that breaks and then becomes a floor): in his view they do not explain what is happening in the market. (01:26–01:47)
- P15.8 He says the series will run across roughly a month ("this entire month"). (01:37–01:47)

- P15.10 To beginners: this may sound daunting, but he says they will be able to understand it. (01:57–02:08)

## P15.3 What trading is — and is not
- P15.11 Most viewers start from "I want to make money, have a better life, be free"; he says that is fine to want but is the wrong way to think when starting trading. (02:42–03:26)
- P15.12 He says making money is not the objective of trading; trading is a skill set (basketball analogy: he played through high school, and says he will give many basketball analogies). (03:26–03:48)
- P15.13 Analogy: LeBron James practising focuses on getting better at the skill, not on the money each basket is worth; money is a side effect of being very good. (03:48–05:01)
- P15.14 Day trading = being able to predict, with high probability and on a daily basis, where the price of any asset class will go. That is how he defines a skilled day trader. (06:46–07:20; repeated 08:05–08:26, 13:18–13:49)
- P15.15 The best side effect of gaining that skill is making money. (07:10–07:20)
- P15.16 The question to ask when you open the charts: "how can I predict where price goes with high probability today", not "how do I make money today". (07:20–07:44)
- P15.17 The series will teach how to combine different confluences into a strategy that predicts where price wants to go, and to do it daily. (07:44–08:15)
- P15.18 He says there is no such thing as being "good at making money": people who make a lot are just very skilled; the worst basketball players make no money from basketball. (08:26–09:29, 13:49–14:22)
- P15.19 Instruction to viewers: write down that day trading is not about making money; when learning a confluence, do not ask whether it will make money today, ask whether it builds your skill. (09:29–10:22)
- P15.20 Money is a tool / energy / leverage that helps you get places, but you cannot make it without skills. (10:33–11:04)
- P15.21 Another definition-style line: the point of the series, the strategy, the psychology discipline and the risk-management strategy is to improve the skill of day trading, not "skill at making money". (13:49–14:10)

## P15.4 Why money-focus is dangerous (his claims)
- P15.22 If you sit down thinking "I want to make money today", he says you may press buy at the open from fear of missing out, put the whole account on the line (leverage the entire account), take trades you should not take, and risk more than you should. He says this can lead to losing money. (05:13–06:46)
- P15.23 Instead you go in with a strategy and take trades from price action on candlestick charts; he says this series is what will teach that. (06:04–06:24)
- P15.24 He says that with a "how do I make money today" mindset you will never be a profitable / net-positive day trader, whatever happens — his claim, unverified. (11:04–11:39)
- P15.25 You must remove the mental attachment to money to succeed; that also keeps you from being focused on money too early, because the skill is difficult to learn. (11:39–12:03)

## P15.5 Expectations he sets for learners
- P15.26 He would be lying if he promised viewers would be millionaires right after the series; the skill is difficult to learn. (11:53–12:13)
- P15.27 "50/50 transaction", which he says he already said on day one: he shows up every day with the videos; the viewer must watch and also take action; he cannot do the work for the viewer. (12:03–12:35)
- P15.28 Practice results: after learning confluences you will not mark them correctly at first (piano analogy: you still play badly the next day but know a few notes and are better than yesterday); keep practising and failing less. (16:06–17:00)
- P15.29 Trading is a skill-based game with no genetics involved; success is about who puts in the most work, so much that becoming (or staying not) profitable is unreasonable to happen; profitability should be the expected result of the work. (17:00–18:03)
- P15.30 His own account: years of work and hours of chart time; when he turned profitable he was not excited, only thinking it was about time. His claim about himself, unverified. (18:03–18:26)
- P15.31 Same attitude to his YouTube milestones (100,000 and 1,000,000 subscribers): not excited because he expected them from the work put in. This is about channel growth, not trading results. (18:26–19:08)
- P15.32 For viewers: the aim is not to be excited about becoming profitable, but to know it is a matter of time given the work. (19:08–19:44)

## P15.6 How to treat wins and losses
- P15.33 He says you should be trading on a demo account while learning, not real money, so a loss costs nothing real. (12:45–13:07)
- P15.34 A loss should make you glad: it is a lesson and room to improve; not sad, mad or disappointed. (13:07–13:18, 14:22–14:45)
- P15.35 A win should not make you happy about the money; look at what you did right and how to repeat it, using your trading journal. (14:45–15:25)
- P15.36 Analyse wins and losses alike: do more of the good, less of the bad; that is how skill grows. (15:25–16:06)
- P15.37 Do not read a loss as being a bad trader: he says you can have 100 green days and one red day; not everybody has a green day every day; do not dwell on the red day or aim at "$500 every day". (19:44–20:50)
- P15.38 He regrets that trading has a direct link between a day's outcome and money (unlike a basketball player with one bad game), and wants viewers to separate the skill from the money. (20:08–21:01)

## P15.7 How skill is measured (previewed, no numbers)
- P15.39 Skill is measured by win rate (how often you win versus lose) and risk-to-reward (how much you risk versus win per trade); combined they show whether you are profitable or skilled. (21:01–21:34)
- P15.40 He says a later video will break this down with data, using online trade journals fed with broker statistics, to optimise risk management, and will show what win rate and risk-to-reward are needed to be profitable consistently. No figures are given here. (21:22–22:05)
- P15.41 First step: replace "I want to make money today" with "I want to get better at trading today". (22:05–22:26)
