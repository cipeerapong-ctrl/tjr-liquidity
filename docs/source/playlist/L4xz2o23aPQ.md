## P5.A Why timing matters
- P5.1 Timing is very important: without understanding the time of the market he cannot trade with high probability on a daily basis; his goal is high-probability decisions each day about where price will go, and ignoring timing hurts that. (00:00–00:33)
- P5.2 Because he trades US indexes (this series teaches S&P 500 and Nasdaq), he is really only looking to trade the New York session. There are three sessions, plus specific times inside New York that are especially valuable. (00:33–00:44, 03:06)

## P5.B The sessions and their times (all Eastern Time)
- P5.3 Asian session: 18:00 to 03:00. (00:44–00:56)
- P5.4 London session: 03:00 to 08:30. (00:56)
- P5.5 Technically London runs until 11:30 New York time, but he ends each session where the next one opens. (01:09)
- P5.6 London pre-market actually opens at 02:00 while the London market open is 03:00. New York pre-market opens 08:30. (01:19)
- P5.7 He marks London's end at 08:30 because pre-market manipulation typically shows up then: new money, US traders, comes into the market. (01:19–01:41)
- P5.8 New York: 08:30 to 09:30 is pre-market, 09:30 is the market open, and the session runs from 09:30 to 17:00. (01:41–02:02)
- P5.9 17:00 to 18:00 is "spread hour": no market is open, and on a CFD, forex or commodities broker the spreads on every pair become very large because no money is in the market. (02:02–02:23)
- P5.10 Why sessions matter (links back to the previous episode's session highs/lows): new money comes into the market at each session and manipulates the previous session's high and low, then reverses toward a draw on liquidity. (02:23–02:54, 04:15–04:36)
- P5.11 He uses prior sessions to spot each session's manipulation and as draws on liquidity, either as a target or as an entry area. (04:15–04:36)

## P5.C The New York time windows
- P5.12 Open = 09:30 ET (he says he operates on Eastern time). (03:06–03:20)
- P5.13 Manipulation window = 09:30 to 09:50. (03:20–03:41)
- P5.14 Entry window = 09:50 to about 10:10. After that "we just carry on". (03:41–03:53)
- P5.15 There is also a PM session starting around 13:00 (1 pm); he personally does not trade it but adds it to the list. (03:53–04:04)
- P5.16 Reason for the windows: the 09:30 open brings new money, so some form of manipulation is expected in New York, whether it targets a higher-time-frame or a lower-time-frame draw on liquidity. (04:36–04:56)
- P5.17 Manipulation usually happens inside 09:30–09:50; sometimes it is done by 09:40 with an entry at 09:45, but typically, on high or low time frames alike, it falls in this window. (04:46–05:17)
- P5.18 The typical entry is 09:50–10:10; he calls this the "macro" and says it is the ideal entry time. (05:17)
- P5.19 For him personally: if he has not found a trade by 10:30 he is done for the day, because the market tends to slow down then. (05:17–05:30)
- P5.20 He says the whole lesson is short and this is all that is needed for the topic; the strategy and which trades to take come later when everything is combined with the other confluences. (05:30–05:41, 06:23–06:35)
