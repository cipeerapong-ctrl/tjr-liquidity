## P14.1 Series framing and goal
- P14.1 Day three is a beginner-level lesson: how to read a candlestick chart on TradingView. Three topics: candlestick anatomy, how to identify highs and lows, how to identify trends. (00:00–00:23)
- P14.2 After the basics, the series moves to the topics he says most viewers came for: liquidity, fair value gaps, advanced liquidity, inverse fair value gaps, advanced imbalance, SMT divergence, time theory (one item between "advanced imbalance" and "SMT" is garbled in the captions and not read). He says these are what help someone become a full-time trader. (00:23–00:45)
- P14.3 Recalls the previous episode: trading = predicting, with high probability and on a daily basis, where price will go; to do that you must be able to see price. (00:45–00:57)
- P14.4 Two kinds of traders: technical analysis (he is one; most of his trading comes from what the chart shows) and fundamental analysis (mostly news, earnings, macro; he names Warren Buffett as a long-term buy-and-hold investor, not a candle-by-candle trader). (00:57–01:59)
- P14.5 The group he teaches actively trades price: buy low and sell high, and sell high and buy back low. He says mistakes are expected; his claim that this is how they will make money is unverified. (02:12–02:34)
- P14.6 He takes no trades from indicators; he uses indicators only to mark his confluences, and promises to cover them later in the series. (03:47–03:57)
- P14.7 Why start here: highs, lows and trends are the bare minimum. He says the real reasons price reacts at a high (for example, price takes a high then falls) come later over the next ~2 weeks; explaining now would confuse. (10:53–11:37)

## P14.3 Candlestick anatomy
- P14.14 Candles on any timeframe (daily, weekly, hourly) together show every second of price movement; he says that without understanding candle anatomy you cannot go further. (11:37–11:58)
- P14.15 Chart types he lists and passes over: bar, hollow candles, volume candles, line, step line, columns, high-low, volume footprint, Heikin-Ashi, Renko. Japanese candlesticks are the most valuable for reading the market because each shows four things. (11:58–12:30)
- P14.16 He draws one up candle and one down candle on the NQ1! daily chart to teach from. (12:30–13:22)
- P14.17 One candle = one period of time (set by the timeframe). It records where price went during the period, where it opened and where it closed. (13:34–13:56)
- P14.18 Four key price points per candle: open, high, low, close. (14:39–14:50, 16:42–17:03)
- P14.19 Parts: the filled rectangle between two lines is the body; the thin lines above and below are the wicks (upper wick, lower wick). He treats a wick as a trail showing price travelled there at some point in the period and then came back. (14:50–16:31)
- P14.20 Up candle: bottom of the body = open; top of the body = close; top of the upper wick = high; bottom of the lower wick = low. (16:31–17:03; frames 16:50–19:10 show these four words typed beside the drawn candle)
- P14.21 Walk-through on a daily candle: a new daily candle opens each 24 hours; price starts at the open, travels to its lowest point and its highest point in the period, then ends at the close. (17:14–19:01)
- P14.22 Down candle: the same four points, but the TOP of the body is the open and the BOTTOM of the body is the close (opened high, closed lower). The wick tips are still the high and the low. In default colours it would be red. (19:01–19:31)
- P14.23 He calls an up candle a "bullish" candle (and a blue candle in his colours); an up candle opened lower and closed higher. The word "bearish" is not said in the captions; he says "down candle". (19:43, 23:31–23:53)
- P14.24 Live check on the real, still-forming daily candle: TradingView shows the time left until the candle closes on the right; the current price is not a close yet. He reads that candle's open, highest point so far, lowest point so far and last price, then the previous day's open, high, low and close. (19:55–22:05)

## P14.4 Timeframes
- P14.25 The timeframe buttons at the top left set how much time each candle covers. He lists 1 second, 5 second, 1 minute, 5 minute, 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week, 1 month (and uses 4 hour and 15 minute below). (13:56–14:16)
- P14.26 On the 1-day timeframe each candle = 24 hours of price action; on 4 hours, each candle = 4 hours. TradingView counts down the time left in the current candle (example: a 4h candle with 1 h 25 min left). (14:16–14:39, 22:17–23:21)
- P14.27 Zooming in: one 4-hour candle is the same price movement as four 1-hour candles; an hour splits into four 15-minute candles; the chart can go to 5 minutes and 1 minute. (24:15–25:51)
- P14.28 Changing timeframe never changes the price movement; it only changes how many candles it is cut into. Smaller timeframe = more candles; bigger timeframe = fewer, larger candles; one daily candle contains a whole day's movement. (25:51–26:45)
- P14.29 Which timeframes he actually uses is deferred to later in the series. (26:45–26:57)
- P14.30 He tells viewers it will become second nature and not to be overwhelmed (basketball dribbling comparison). (27:08–27:59)

## P14.5 Highs and lows
- P14.31 Why it matters: highs and lows are "very key" in his strategy and in predicting price with high probability on a daily basis. Trends and "order flow" are also named as things to understand, but order flow is not explained in this clip. (28:10–28:42)
- P14.32 Idea: price cannot go up forever; a peak needs a rise followed by a fall. Same for a low: price cannot fall forever; it ends with a rise. He draws a "^" shape and a "V" shape (frames 30:10–32:10). (28:53–30:05)
- P14.33 Definition (move level): a HIGH = a move up followed by a move down. A LOW = a move down followed by a move up. (30:18–31:02)
- P14.34 His claim that learning this will earn more than a college education is unverified. (30:40)
- P14.35 Definition (candle level): a HIGH = an up candle followed by a down candle. A LOW = a down candle followed by an up candle. (32:08–32:30)
- P14.36 Where the level is: for a high, take the highest point of the two candles (the one with the higher wick). For a low, take the lowest point of the two candles (whichever wick is lower). He marks several examples on the live NQ1! daily chart. (32:41–33:45)
- P14.37 Highs and lows matter because they make up trends. (31:02–31:14)

## P14.6 Trends
- P14.38 Trends move the market. The market can move three ways: up, down or sideways ("consolidation" is named once). He draws one arrow for each (frames 31:30–31:50). He says highs and lows are how to tell which of the three is happening. (31:14–31:58)
- P14.39 Uptrend = higher highs AND higher lows. Sequence he describes: a high, then a low, then a higher high, then a higher low, repeating. (34:10–34:53)
- P14.40 Real uptrend example: the S&P 500 over a long span; frames show SPX on the monthly timeframe with small marks at lows and highs. He reads off low, higher low, high, higher high… and says the COVID drop is explained later. His reason people invest in the index (it keeps rising on high timeframes) is his comment. (34:53–35:55)
- P14.41 Downtrend = lower highs AND lower lows. Sequence: a low, then a high, then a lower low, then a lower high, repeating. (36:06–36:39)
- P14.42 Downtrend example: ES1! (S&P 500 E-mini) on 4h; he marks a high, a low, then lower low, lower high, lower low, lower high, and a lower low forming at the right edge. He tells viewers to ignore the rest of the chart for now; break of structure and how new trends form come later. (36:54–38:00)
- P14.43 A trend can break or change. Being in an uptrend does not mean "press buy", and being in a downtrend does not mean "press sell". The point of learning trends is to read them as part of predicting where price wants to go with consistency. (38:10–38:44)

## P14.7 Homework and wrap-up
- P14.44 Homework: (1) set up TradingView, pick chart colours, set New York time (he stresses this repeatedly; for the rest of the series); (2) switch timeframes (for example 4h to 1h shows four 1-hour candles) and practise naming the open, high, low and close of each candle, at least five reps, more is better; (3) find five highs and five lows (a pair of candles, then the extreme point of the two); (4) find five uptrends (higher highs and higher lows) and five downtrends (lower highs and lower lows). (38:55–40:41)
