## P8.1 Where this sits in the series
- P8.1 Recap order: fair value gaps (FVG) were taught two days earlier, advanced imbalance concepts the day before; today is inverse fair value gaps (IFVG). (00:00–00:12)
- P8.2 He now starts classifying each confluence taught so far by the role it plays. (00:24–00:35)
- P8.3 Liquidity = the "reversal confluence": price has the opportunity to fill orders when it pushes above or below a draw on liquidity (a high in an uptrend, or a higher-time-frame high), which can start a reversal. Liquidity is step one of his strategy; he will not go into the strategy in depth yet. (00:35–01:20)
- P8.4 He prefers selling tops and buying bottoms of every move because that gives better risk-to-reward, higher odds and better chances of being right about the overall move. (01:20–01:41)
- P8.5 Break of structure (BOS) = a "confirmation confluence": price pushed above an area where orders could fill, and the break of structure in the other direction confirms that orders were filled above that high (above the sweep). (01:41–02:36)
- P8.6 Liquidity only gives price the opportunity to fill orders above highs / below lows; it does not force price to. A change in trend, shown by a break of structure, is how he confirms price took the opportunity and wants to reverse. (02:26–02:47)
- P8.7 At this point in the series BOS is the only confirmation confluence he has taught; fair value gaps are the continuation confluence (taught two days earlier). (02:47–03:00)

## P8.2 Why a continuation confluence is wanted after confirmation
- P8.8 Chain for a downtrend entry: price pushes above a high (opportunity to fill orders), breaks structure down (confirmation), then needs proof the new trend will continue. (03:00–03:33)
- P8.9 He is "extra safe" and says students should be too, since real money will be at risk and the strategy has to be airtight. He will not trade just because price pushed above a high and broke structure down, because that could be only a higher-time-frame retrace and price could run all the way back up. (03:20–03:51)
- P8.10 So he needs continuation: a fair value gap in the new downtrend; price pushes up into it and then continues down. Opportunity to fill orders, confirmation that orders filled, confirmation that the trend continues; he calls this a very optimal entry for targeting other draws on liquidity to the left. (03:51–04:43)

## P8.3 The inverse fair value gap — his definition
- P8.11 Today's new item is a SECOND confirmation confluence, and it is built from fair value gaps. A fair value gap is supposed to show continuation: price fills it, then pushes on in the trend direction. (04:43–05:13)
- P8.12 If a trend does not continue, price is starting a new trend. His question: what if a fair value gap inside a current uptrend is disrespected, meaning price comes down and CLOSES UNDERNEATH it? (05:13–05:36)
- P8.13 That is not what a continuation confluence is supposed to do (price should come into it and move higher). Closing through it suggests price is changing direction, so it gives a confirmation confluence of a change in trend. That is all an inverse fair value gap is. (05:25–05:56)
- P8.14 Definition, paraphrased: an inverse fair value gap is a fair value gap that gets disrespected within the trend it sits in, and it can be used as a break of structure, i.e. as a confirmation confluence. (05:56–06:18)
- P8.15 Direction: bullish gap in an uptrend closed under = change to a downtrend (bearish inverse, he states this case in words at 05:13–05:56 and shows it at 07:59–09:29). Bearish gap in a downtrend closed above = change to an uptrend (bullish inverse; shown at 06:38–07:41, 11:45–12:25, 20:14–21:26). (05:13–05:56; 06:38–12:25; 20:14–21:26)
- P8.16 Role change: the gap that was a continuation confluence turns into a confirmation confluence once it is inversed. (07:19–07:31, 21:16–21:36)
- P8.17 How to find a fair value gap is not re-taught here (already covered in the FVG episode). (05:56)
- P8.18 Link to "orders were filled": the explicit sentence "orders were filled above the sweep" is said about the break of structure (P8.5). He then says the inverse gap serves as the same kind of confirmation confluence (change in trend), so its job is the same; he does not repeat the "orders filled" sentence for the inverse gap itself. (02:15–02:36; 05:46–06:18; 21:26–21:36)

## P8.4 What counts as "inversed" — the close rule
- P8.19 The gap is invalidated only on a full candle CLOSE beyond the far side of the gap: underneath the bottom of a bullish gap (09:08–09:18), above the top of a bearish gap (20:44–20:55). Not on the candle that merely enters or wicks through it. (09:08–09:18, 20:34–20:55)
- P8.20 A wick pushed above a bearish gap is not an inverse, and neither is a candle that closes INSIDE the gap; it counts only once a candle closes fully above the gap. He ties this to the break-of-structure rule: wait for a full candle close. (20:34–21:06)
- P8.21 Time frame: every example is on the 4-hour chart (ES1!). He never states a general rule for which time frame an IFVG must be on, so "4-hour" is only what he demonstrates; the candle in "candle close" is the 4-hour candle in these examples. (06:28–06:48; frames throughout)

## P8.5 Chart examples (all ES1! 4h, historical)
- P8.22 Example 1 (bullish inverse; frames show Dec 2025 price): price broke below the most recent low with a close under it (downtrend); a bearish fair value gap formed; price rose into it and closed above it, inversing the continuation confluence that should have sent price lower. Price then moved up slowly, making higher highs and higher lows. (06:38–07:41)
- P8.23 Example 2 (bearish inverse; frames show late Aug 2025 price): price had closed above the most recent high, confirming an uptrend; the low that would be monitored for a break of structure was still intact. A bullish fair value gap sat below, the last imbalance price needed to tap to continue higher. Frames show the gap as a grey rectangle drawn over the gap candles, and a black curved mark rising out of the rectangle at about 08:45–09:00, matching his "if respected, price continues higher" sentence. (07:59–08:56)
- P8.24 The gap is not invalidated on the first candle into it; it is invalidated on the candle that closes below the bottom of the bullish gap. Once that candle closes he says price is probably turning to a downtrend, and it did. (09:08–09:29)
- P8.25 Benefit: with only break of structure he would have waited for another 4-hour candle to close under the low. With the inverse gap he could enter earlier, which he calls an extra 68 ticks. (09:29–09:59)
- P8.26 Risk-to-reward comparison on that same short, drawn with TradingView's short-position tool (frames, ~09:55–10:50): entering at the close that inverses the gap gives a risk-to-reward of about 1:1.3 (stop about 27.75 points = 111 ticks, target about 36 points below the entry) and, with a deeper target he mentions next, about 1:2.35 on the label. Entering at the break of structure instead gives about 1:0.45 (stop about 44 points = 176 ticks, target about 19.75 points); he says it makes the stop about twice as large (the frames show about 1.6 times). The 65-tick difference between the two stops is close to his "68 ticks" (P8.25). In dollar terms, risking $1,000: about $450 won versus about $1,300. These are one-chart illustrations, not results; the captions garble the second ratio ("1 to 2"), the frame label reads about 2.35. (09:59–11:02)
- P8.27 His reasoning: executing at the inverse close gives better risk-to-reward and, he says, a better win rate; no win-rate number is given. (09:49–10:09)
- P8.28 Example 3 (bullish inverse; frames show Nov 2025 price): a bearish gap; price closes above it; the high above was the one he was monitoring for a break of structure. Waiting for the BOS needed another 4-hour candle, costing about 123 ticks versus entering on the inverse. (11:45–12:25)
- P8.29 He says he uses this confluence almost every day, "almost more than" break of structure, because it usually happens before the break of structure does. (11:23–11:45; 12:15–12:25)

## P8.7 Stacked fair value gaps — which gap must be inversed
- P8.31 Context: in the FVG episode he said that with stacked gaps all are valid and price chooses which to push from; once price pushes off one, the gaps below are no longer needed. (12:45–13:16)
- P8.32 Stack = a gap followed immediately by another gap with no retrace candle between them; the two are grouped together as one imbalanced range. (13:57–14:18)
- P8.33 Reasoning: price can come into the stack and push higher; or it can close under the upper gap, come down into the lower gap and push higher from there. (14:40–15:22)
- P8.34 Rule: for an inverse to the DOWNSIDE out of a stack of bullish gaps, the BOTTOM gap must be inversed (closed under). A close under only the upper gap means nothing, because price can still fill the lower gap and continue; the lower gap is the last one holding up the trend. If the upper gap is inversed it is not a problem, but if the bottom one is, "the trend is gone". (15:22–16:51)
- P8.35 First stack example (frames show ES1! 4h around early May 2025: a thin upper gap and a grey rectangle below it): price closed under both gaps, only barely under the lower one, then fell for a while. He wishes that candle had ended right at the lower gap for a cleaner example. (13:36–17:04)
- P8.36 Second stack example (frames show April 2025: a small upper gap and a large grey gap below): a close under only the top gap does not make a full inverse and the uptrend stays intact (known because price closed above the most recent high); he would have to wait for a close under the lower gap, which he notes would take a long time. (17:04–18:42)
- P8.37 He states the stack rule only for downside inverses (bottom gap). The upside mirror (top gap of a stack of bearish gaps) is not spelled out. (15:32–15:44)
