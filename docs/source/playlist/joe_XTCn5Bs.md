## P7.1 What equilibrium is
- P7.1 Equilibrium is a continuation confluence: it shows the opportunity for the current trend to continue. He opens by calling it the easiest confluence, yet says people keep marking it incorrectly. (00:00–00:56)
- P7.2 He uses a range-box tool (he calls it a "Gann box") to find it and says he will show his settings so viewers can copy them. The settings are never shown (no settings dialog in the frames, 01:04–01:52). (00:56, 01:28)
- P7.3 Purpose: equilibrium splits the range of the current trend into a premium range and a discount range. (01:06–01:17, 01:49–02:00)
- P7.4 Definition, boiled down: equilibrium is the 50% mark from the most recent swing low to the most recent swing high within the trend. (03:05–03:16)
- P7.5 On screen the tool is a rectangle with three levels labelled 0, 0.5 and 1; the 0.5 line (tinted green on the 15m charts) is equilibrium. Uptrend: 0 sits on the low and 1 on the high. Downtrend: 0 on the high and 1 on the low. (frames 01:04–01:28, 06:16, 07:16, 11:52)

## P7.2 Premium and discount in an uptrend
- P7.6 Picture: a move up, a move down, a move up; when the up-extension finishes, price sits in the premium. (01:28–01:49)
- P7.7 For a buy inside that uptrend, he does not want to buy anything above the equilibrium line. Below it is the discounted price range and above it the premium, both measured from the swing low to the swing high. (02:00–02:21)
- P7.8 Market-maker reasoning: they are not going to buy at the top (premium); they want price pushed down into a discount, buy up the shares there, and then push price higher. (02:32–02:53)
- P7.9 So when price falls and pushes past equilibrium into the discount, that is where he looks for potential buys, and the market makers want the same. (02:53–03:05)
- P7.10 Use as a confluence: once price has gone under equilibrium and bullish confluences appear, buy orders were filled down there; price then trades back into the premium and probably continues the trend past the high. (03:16–03:37)

## P7.3 Where it sits in the full sequence
- P7.11 Chain he walks through: (1) liquidity = the chance to fill orders (price pushes above a draw on liquidity); (2) confirmation that orders were filled = an inverse fair value gap (price does not continue the uptrend); (3) continuation confluence = a fair value gap OR equilibrium; (4) take the trade in the direction of the new trend. (03:37–04:48)
- P7.12 In that example price comes back up into the range and then moves down out of it; shorts are taken off that. He words the range as "discounted" here, which is muddled; see P7.17. (04:18–04:48)
- P7.13 Why shorts there: there was an opportunity to fill orders, a confirmation confluence showed they were filled, and he now waits for the trend that is forming to continue. (04:38–04:48)

## P7.4 How to draw it (the rule he shouts about)
- P7.14 Uptrend: draw from the MOST RECENT low up to the MOST RECENT high. Not from an older low that merely connects to a high. (04:48–05:43)
- P7.15 He repeats it with chart pointing: most recent low to most recent high, "you are just following the trend". (05:43–06:51)
- P7.16 Downtrend: the mirror, from the most recent high down to the most recent low; not from an older high. (06:51–07:15)
- P7.17 In a downtrend price retraces UP into the range, which he calls the premium; that is where he wants to short, because he wants price to go lower. He adds that the premium "is actually the discount" for this purpose; the auto-caption wording is garbled, so the exact meaning of that phrase is not resolved. On the 15m frames the retrace goes up through the 0.5 line into the upper half of the high-to-low box. (07:15–07:46; frames 11:40–12:16)
