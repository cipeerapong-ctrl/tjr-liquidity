## P1.3 The strategy as a chain of ideas
- P1.17 Core logic in one line: orders are filled → change in order flow → continuation of the new trend → entry, with the target at other (or internal) draws on liquidity. (10:32–10:53)
- P1.18 Step logic in his words: orders have been filled or have the potential to be filled; he knows they were filled by a change in market structure or order flow; he knows the change will hold by seeing continuation; the exit target is where those filled orders can be exited, a draw on liquidity or an imbalance. (10:43–11:23)
- P1.19 Final four-part summary: (1) potential for orders to fill = a liquidity sweep of a significant high or low; (2) confirmation orders were filled = confirmation confluences (lower-time-frame break of structure or inverse fair value gap); (3) continuation of the new trend = continuation confluences (equilibrium or a fair value gap); (4) exit at possible liquidation points. (35:44–36:46, 38:17–38:50, 40:53–41:35)
- P1.20 Exit zones he lists: higher-time-frame highs, higher-time-frame lows, previous session highs, previous session lows. (41:14–41:35)
- P1.21 He says the strategy "makes sense" because it is about liquidity: opportunity to fill orders, confirmation they were filled, continuation of the trend, exit where they can be liquidated. He is trading with the inflows and outflows of the market. (41:24–41:56)
- P1.22 He is not a fan of support and resistance; he compares trading "bounce off a floor to a ceiling" to flipping a coin, while noting some people do succeed with it. (41:56–42:26)

## General definitions (web, not TJR)
- Smart-money divergence (SMT): a disagreement between correlated instruments, e.g. S&P 500 and Nasdaq, where one makes a higher high / lower low and the other fails to. Source: https://www.tradingview.com/ideas/smartmoneydivergence (trader education, not authoritative).
- Fair value gap (FVG): a three-candle imbalance where the first and third candle wicks do not overlap; its midpoint is often treated as a key level. Source: https://ninjatrader.com/futures/blogs/fair-value-gaps/
- Inverse FVG: a gap that has been fully traded through and then flips role; sources are thin. Source: https://www.tradingview.com/ideas/smartmoneydivergence (indicator pages only). TJR's own use is what the clip shows, not these definitions.
