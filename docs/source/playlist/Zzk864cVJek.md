## P11.2 Market structure (the base for the definition)
- P11.3 Uptrend = higher highs and higher lows. Downtrend = lower highs and lower lows. He calls this pattern of highs and lows market structure. (0:44–1:06)

## P11.3 What a break of structure is
- P11.5 A break of structure = the current market structure that price is in breaks. (1:28–1:39)
- P11.6 Breaking bullish structure (an uptrend): price must break below the higher low, make a lower low, then a lower high. He calls that a market structure shift and a break of structure. (1:39–2:12)
- P11.7 Breaking bearish structure (a downtrend), the mirror: price must push above a high, make a higher high, then a higher low. He calls that a shift in market structure to the upside. (2:12–2:33)
- P11.8 Practical identification on the chart: while the chart shows higher highs and higher lows, the only thing to watch is the MOST RECENT low; the break happens when a candle CLOSES underneath it. (2:33–3:06)
- P11.9 Mirror for a downtrend: the break to the upside needs a candle closure above the MOST RECENT high. He stresses "most recent": an older low or high further back is not the one to watch. (3:06–3:27)

## P11.4 Wick vs close (his rule)
- P11.10 A candle whose body only ends level with the low does not count; the body must close underneath the lowest point of the most recent low. He calls this S&P 500 example a hard one to read. Frames: ES1! 5m with a horizontal ray at the swing low; which candle body sits level with it is too small to confirm on the 480p video, so only the caption is relied on. (4:23–4:54)
- P11.11 When it did close below, that candle is the break of structure; from there price is in a downtrend (lower high, lower low, lower high). (4:54–5:15)
- P11.12 A wick above a high does not count either: if a candle only puts a wick above the most recent high, he does NOT press buy; wait until a full candle closes above the most recent high, and only then is order flow shifted to the upside. (6:17–6:49)
- P11.13 In the longest example (uptrend) one candle's wick goes drastically below the monitored low; he says loudly that this is not a break of structure because there is no close underneath the low. - P11.14 After that wick, price still counts as an uptrend even though it printed a lower high and a lower low; the low to monitor becomes the new most recent low, and the break came only on a candle closing underneath it. (8:55–9:25)

## P11.5 What does NOT count
- P11.15 In a downtrend, a higher low alone does not break structure: you need a candle closing above the most recent high. (5:46–6:17)
- P11.16 In an uptrend, a lower high alone does not break structure. For a break to the downside you watch only the lows, specifically the most recent lows formed within the trend; the highs are not monitored. (7:00–8:02)
- P11.17 Procedure he repeats: after each move down then up, the newest low becomes the one monitored; if the next candles do not close under it, structure stands. (8:02–8:22, 8:55–9:15)

## General definitions (web, not TJR)
- Break of structure in common usage: a candle close beyond the most recent swing high (uptrend) or swing low (downtrend). The source notes that close-based confirmation filters out wick-only liquidity sweeps at the cost of later entries, that conventions differ, and that the term is commonly described as a trend-CONTINUATION signal (a break in the direction of the trend). TJR uses the term for a trend CHANGE (a break against the current trend). Source: https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/break-of-structure/ (educational, not authoritative); see also https://www.tmgm.com/en/academy/trading-academy/break-of-structure
